About the project

Follow the money, not just the account.

Instant payment systems such as UPI settle in seconds and cannot easily be reversed. Fraudsters use this by moving stolen money through chains of “mule” accounts, often held by ordinary people who lend or sell their accounts, before it is cashed out. By the time a victim reports the fraud, the money has usually moved on.

Most detection looks at one account or one transaction at a time, but mule activity shows up in the network: money fanning out to many accounts, converging again, or circling back, with very short gaps between receiving and forwarding. Rules strict enough to catch this also fire on legitimate behaviour such as shopkeepers receiving many payments, salary payouts, rent collection and group expense splits. MuleNet-Graph looks at the shape of the money flow instead.

Approach

How it works

  • Rolling graph: payments stream into a graph of accounts and transfers covering the last ten minutes.
  • Local neighbourhood: for each new transaction, a small bounded neighbourhood around the accounts involved is extracted.
  • Structure and speed features: fan-out, fan-in, circular flow, pass-through ratio and the gap between receiving and forwarding money.
  • Scoring: a graph neural network (GraphSAGE or GAT) is designed to score the neighbourhood. Until it is trained, the system runs a graph-based heuristic stand-in, and the dashboard says so.
  • Graded response: monitor, flag, hold outgoing transfers, or freeze and escalate to a human reviewer. Final decisions stay with people.
  • Explanation: each alert shows the rules that fired, the node’s features, and which transfers most changed its score when removed.

System

Components

Synthetic data generatorNormal traffic, injectable mule rings (fan-out, fan-in, circular) and hard negatives such as shopkeepers, salary, rent, group splits and friend settlements.
Graph and featuresRolling NetworkX graph, bounded k-hop extraction, velocity and structure features.
BaselinesSingle-account rules and a graph-based heuristic, used for comparison.
Streaming backendIn-process queue and state store (Kafka and Redis optional), decision engine, FastAPI REST and WebSocket.
DashboardReact and Cytoscape.js: live graph, attack launcher, threshold sliders, event log and explanation panel.

Results so far

Synthetic data only

Offline replay, default configuration, seed 42. “Hard-negative FPR” is the share of legitimate look-alike scenarios in which at least one account was alerted.

Single-account rulesPrecision 0.87 · Recall 0.61 · F1 0.72 · Hard-negative FPR 0.33
Graph heuristicPrecision 0.98 · Recall 0.83 · F1 0.90 · Hard-negative FPR 0.03
Graph neural networkImplemented, not yet trained or benchmarked. No accuracy claim is made.

Single-account rules false-alarmed on every salary scenario and 62% of shopkeeper scenarios. The graph heuristic false-alarmed on 12% of salary scenarios and none of the shopkeeper ones. Both caught all injected rings before cash-out in this replay, with a median delay of about 24 to 27 simulated seconds.

Honest status: this is a research prototype built and tested entirely on synthetic data, so results are likely optimistic. The graph neural network has not been trained, and the 50 ms latency goal is unverified. It is meant to complement existing tools such as MuleHunter.AI, FRI and the I4C Suspect Registry, not replace them. Flagged accounts are risk nodes for human review, not accusations. If the live demo shows “disconnected”, wait about a minute for the backend to wake up and refresh the page.

Next steps

What comes next

  • Train and benchmark the graph neural network against the baselines, and measure real inference latency.
  • Add harder test data: slower, adaptive rings and more legitimate look-alikes.
  • Validate on privacy-protected transaction data through an institutional partner.
  • Test explanations and the review workflow with fraud analysts.
  • Run a monitor-only pilot before any account restrictions are enabled.
